Europe

Species — Europe
Continental Europe, UK, Balkans Snake — Viperidae Hemotoxic + Neurotoxic (variable)
European Vipers
Vipera berus (common European adder — the only venomous snake native to the UK), V. aspis (asp viper — France, Italy, Switzerland), and V. ammodytes (nose-horned viper — the Balkans, most dangerous of the three). One antivenom family covers all three, but severity differs meaningfully by species and geography
Onset / Timeline
Local pain and swelling develop over the first hours. Most bites across this genus stay mild-to-moderate; a genuine minority progress to systemic involvement, and that minority is meaningfully larger and more severe for V. ammodytes than for V. berus.
Clinical Syndrome
Progressive local swelling, vomiting, tachycardia, hypotension, and — in more severe cases — acute kidney injury, hemorrhage, angioedema, pulmonary edema, and rarely cardiac arrest or neurotoxicity. V. ammodytes stands apart within the genus: in the largest published case series (Croatia, >500 confirmed bites), roughly 16-22% of victims developed local or systemic complications, with neurotoxic findings (ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, dysphagia, dysphonia, neuromuscular weakness) in about 15-16% of cases — genuinely uncommon for a European viper but well short of rare. Fatalities from V. ammodytes are documented, though concentrated in children and hypersensitive individuals rather than typical healthy adults.
Field / Prehospital Management
Standard viper approach: immobilize the limb, remove constricting items, transport. No tourniquet, incision, or suction.
ED / Resuscitation Management
Serial limb assessment and monitoring for systemic signs. In the UK, decisions on antivenom are guided by the National Poison Information Service via TOXBASE rather than a fixed local protocol — involve regional poison control early given how genuinely uncommon these bites are for most clinicians to have direct experience with.
Antivenom
ViperaTAb (MicroPharm, UK, ovine Fab, monospecific against V. berus) and Viperfav (MicroPharm/France, similar Fab product) both show cross-reactivity across European Vipera species in animal models and supporting clinical data, including published use for V. aspis and V. ammodytes envenomation. In UK audit data, 20-60 of an estimated 50-200 annual V. berus bites are treated with antivenom, given IV, with better outcomes reported for IV Fab products (ViperaTAb, Viperfav) compared with older intramuscular antivenoms (Zagreb, Biomed, Bulbio) still used in parts of continental Europe. A single IV dose of Viperfav has shown good efficacy and pharmacokinetics for V. ammodytes specifically when given within hours of the bite.
Disposition
Admit for observation and antivenom consideration per regional poison control guidance for any bite with progressive local swelling or systemic signs. Given the neurotoxic minority within V. ammodytes bites specifically, maintain a lower threshold for airway/respiratory monitoring with confirmed or suspected bites from that species and geography.
Pearls / Pitfalls
"Low severity" describes the population, not the individual patient — a genuine subset of bites from any of these three species need real antivenom and monitoring, and V. ammodytes in particular should not be mentally filed alongside the milder V. berus. Older intramuscular antivenoms (Zagreb, Biomed, Bulbio) still in regional use have weaker outcome data than the newer IV Fab products — know which product your local supply chain actually carries.
Non-Critical — Supportive Care Only
Not life-threatening and doesn't require antivenom or critical-care intervention. Not a full workup — just enough to recognize and reassure.
  • Noble False Widow Spider (Steatoda nobilis) — the only medically significant spider in the UK/Ireland, frequently sensationalized in local media as "flesh-eating." Confirmed case series show sharp local pain, erythema, and swelling resolving fully within 48-72 hours with no lasting complications; venom does share Latrodectus-like toxin components but at far lower potency than true widows. Care: analgesia, ice, wound hygiene; the rare reports of severe necrosis or sepsis are almost always secondary bacterial infection from a dirty wound, not the venom itself — clean the bite site and this risk drops accordingly.
  • European Scorpion (Euscorpius spp.) — small, low-potency scorpions found across Southern Europe; sting severity is comparable to a bee sting for the overwhelming majority of cases. Care: ice, analgesia, reassurance; no antivenom exists or is needed.
