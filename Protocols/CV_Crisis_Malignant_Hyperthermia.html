01

Emergency Management & Immediate Actions

Sequence matters less than speed — get help, stop the trigger, and get dantrolene running. Steps below are typically executed in parallel by a full team, not strictly serially.
1
Stop all triggering agents
Turn off volatile agent, switch to TIVA (propofol), do not give further succinylcholine. Continue the case only if it must be finished; convert to IV anesthesia.
2
Call for help and the MH cart
Declare the emergency, get extra hands (dantrolene reconstitution is labor-intensive), and call the MHAUS Hotline 1-800-644-9737 for real-time consultation.
3
Hyperventilate with 100% O₂
Flows of 10 L/min, high minute ventilation to flush residual volatile agent and blow off CO₂. Change to a clean circuit or insert activated-charcoal filters on inspiratory and expiratory limbs if immediately available; replace filters hourly.
4
Dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg IV, rapid bolus
Through the largest available IV. Repeat 2.5 mg/kg boluses every 5–10 minutes until ETCO₂, rigidity, and heart rate improve. No fixed ceiling — doses of 10–20+ mg/kg are occasionally required; reassess the diagnosis if no response at all after ~10 mg/kg.
5
Cool the patient
Cold IV crystalloid, surface ice packs to axillae/groin/neck, cold lavage of open cavities if surgical field allows. Stop active cooling around 38–38.5°C to avoid overshoot. Cooling is adjunctive — dantrolene is the priority.
6
Treat hyperkalemia
IV calcium chloride 10–30 mg/kg for membrane stabilization if arrhythmia/ECG changes; insulin 10 U regular in 50 mL D50, titrated; hyperventilation and bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg if no ABG available. Avoid calcium channel blockers — case reports describe fatal interaction with dantrolene.
7
Treat arrhythmias with standard agents
Most arrhythmias resolve as acidosis and hyperkalemia correct. Use standard antiarrhythmics for persistent/life-threatening rhythms; again, avoid CCBs.
8
Protect the kidneys
Target urine output >2 mL/kg/hr with hydration ± mannitol/furosemide — each dantrolene vial already contains 3 g mannitol, so track cumulative dose before adding more. Send urine for myoglobin.
9
Monitor and reassess
Arterial line, core temperature probe, Foley. Serial ABG, CK, K⁺, lactate, coagulation panel roughly every 15 minutes during the acute phase.
10
Admit to ICU
Minimum 24 hours of observation for recrudescence, with maintenance dantrolene continuing through that window (see Rationale, below).
Do not delay dantrolene for confirmation

MH is a clinical diagnosis made in real time. Waiting on labs before treating measurably worsens outcomes — treat first on the pattern of rising ETCO₂, rigidity, and tachycardia.

02

Brief Review

Malignant hyperthermia (MH) is a rare, life-threatening pharmacogenetic disorder of skeletal muscle. In susceptible individuals, exposure to a volatile anesthetic and/or succinylcholine triggers uncontrolled calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum through a defective ryanodine receptor (RYR1), producing sustained muscle contraction, a hypermetabolic state, and progressive heat production.

Reported incidence of a clinical MH event ranges roughly from 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 50,000–100,000 general anesthetics using triggering agents, with wide variation by population and genetic background; the true prevalence of MH susceptibility (genotype) is higher than the rate of clinical events, since not every exposure in a susceptible patient produces a full crisis. Untreated, MH is fatal in the majority of cases; with early dantrolene, mortality drops to under 5%.

Room Set-Up for the MH-Susceptible Patient

  • Non-triggering technique: total IV anesthesia (propofol-based), no volatile agents, no succinylcholine. Regional/neuraxial anesthesia is not a trigger and is a reasonable option when the case allows it.
  • Prepare the machine: either use a machine with no history of volatile agent, or flush thoroughly with high-flow O₂ per your machine's specific washout protocol, or place activated-charcoal filters (e.g. Vapor-Clean) on inspiratory and expiratory limbs — filters saturate after roughly an hour and need hourly replacement.
  • Dantrolene immediately available: confirm the MH cart is stocked and physically nearby before induction — MHAUS recommends a minimum of 36 vials of Dantrium/Revonto (20 mg each) or 3 vials of Ryanodex (250 mg each) on hand.
  • Monitoring: standard ASA monitors plus continuous core temperature from induction — temperature is not routinely monitored in every case, but is mandatory here.
  • Cooling supplies staged: cold IV fluids and ice readily accessible, not something to send for after a crisis starts.
  • Scheduling: first case of the day when possible, to minimize residual volatile agent contamination in the room and give the team maximum bandwidth if a crisis develops.
  • Team briefing: state the MH-susceptible status explicitly during the pre-induction time-out, not just in the chart.

Rationale for MH Management Steps

The "why" behind the protocol — the physiology that makes each step non-negotiable.

Root cause: a defective RYR1 (or, less commonly, CACNA1S) channel allows uncontrolled calcium efflux from the sarcoplasmic reticulum into the myoplasm. Free calcium drives continuous actin-myosin cross-bridging — this is what produces rigidity, and it is also what produces heat: sustained muscle work is enormously energy-inefficient, and essentially all of that energy dissipates as heat and CO₂.

Why dantrolene works when nothing else does: dantrolene binds RYR1 directly and reduces calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, uncoupling excitation from contraction at the source. Cooling, hyperventilation, and hyperkalemia treatment all manage downstream consequences; dantrolene is the only intervention that turns off the underlying process.

Why cooling is secondary, not first: aggressive thermal management without adequate dantrolene and correction of hypercarbia/acidosis treats a symptom while the metabolic engine keeps running. Current expert opinion (echoed in MHAUS guidance) is to prioritize dantrolene and ventilation/acid-base correction before investing heavily in cooling logistics.

Why calcium channel blockers are avoided: case reports describe severe hyperkalemia and cardiovascular collapse when CCBs are combined with dantrolene, plausibly via interaction with calcium handling that dantrolene itself is trying to correct — the mechanism isn't fully settled, but the clinical signal is consistent enough that CCBs are avoided as a category.

Why maintenance dosing continues for 24 hours: recrudescence — recurrence of MH signs after apparent initial control — occurs in roughly 20% of treated patients. Median time to recrudescence is around 9 hours, with 80% occurring by 16 hours, which is why observation and maintenance dantrolene extend well past the point the crisis appears resolved.

Differential Diagnosis

MimicKey distinguishing features
Neuroleptic malignant syndromeDevelops over days on antipsychotics, not minutes-to-hours after a trigger agent; CK rise is slower; doesn't respond as rapidly to dantrolene as true MH.
Serotonin syndromeClonus and hyperreflexia more prominent than sustained rigidity; history of serotonergic drugs; rigidity itself is comparatively rare.
Thyroid stormNo temporal link to anesthetic trigger; look for known thyroid disease, exophthalmos, prior labs.
Sepsis / occult infectionSlower onset, hemodynamics more distributive (warm, vasodilated) than the hypermetabolic picture of MH.
Pheochromocytoma crisisExtreme, labile hypertension is more dominant than in MH; catecholamine surge pattern.
Inadequate anesthesia / light planeRigidity resolves promptly with deepening anesthesia or a nondepolarizing paralytic — true MH rigidity does not.
Sympathomimetic toxicity (e.g. cocaine)History/exposure driven, no relationship to volatile agent or succinylcholine administration.
Anesthesia machine or environmental overheatingRare, but check warming devices, ambient temperature, and drapes before assuming MH if the rest of the picture doesn't fit.
08

Confirmatory Workup & Disposition

Acute phase: no lab confirms MH in real time — treatment is empiric. Once stabilized, definitive diagnosis requires referral to an MH testing center for the caffeine-halothane contracture test (CHCT), the current gold standard, performed on a fresh muscle biopsy.

Genetic testing: targeted RYR1 (and CACNA1S) panel testing can identify a causative variant in known MH families and spares relatives an invasive biopsy if a familial variant is confirmed; a negative genetic panel does not exclude susceptibility, since not all causative variants are known.

Disposition: ICU admission for a minimum of 24 hours with continuous monitoring; maintenance dantrolene 1 mg/kg IV every 4–6 hours (or infusion around 0.25 mg/kg/hr in fulminant cases) continued until the patient has been stable and improving for 24 hours.

Longitudinal care: patient and family education, referral for CHCT/genetic testing, MedicAlert bracelet, documentation in the anesthesia record and problem list, and cascade screening discussion for first-degree relatives.

MHAUS Hotline — 1-800-644-9737 (outside the U.S.: 001-209-417-3722). Available for real-time crisis consultation and post-event guidance; call during the event, not just after.
09

MH Genetics

MH susceptibility is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern with variable and incomplete penetrance — a positive family history matters even without a documented prior event, and a negative family history does not exclude risk (de novo variants occur).

  • RYR1 (ryanodine receptor 1, chromosome 19q13.1) accounts for the large majority of identified causative variants in MH-susceptible families.
  • CACNA1S (the skeletal muscle L-type calcium channel α1 subunit) accounts for a smaller minority of cases.
  • Over 200 RYR1 variants have been described; only a subset are considered functionally confirmed as causative, which is part of why genetic testing complements — but doesn't replace — the CHCT.
  • MH susceptibility overlaps genetically and clinically with certain congenital myopathies, most notably central core disease, which also maps to RYR1.
  • First-degree relatives of a confirmed or strongly suspected MH patient should be counseled as presumptively susceptible until tested, and managed with a non-triggering technique for any anesthetic in the meantime.
