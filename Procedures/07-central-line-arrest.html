Central Line Placement Under Arrest Conditions

Femoral-first access during active resuscitation — landmark or ultrasound-guided, without interrupting compressions.

VASCULAR ACCESS / CIRCULATORY
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Need for reliable central access during cardiac arrest or peri-arrest resuscitation when IO/peripheral access is inadequate or unavailable
  • High-dose vasopressor administration, rapid volume resuscitation, or need for central venous monitoring during ongoing resuscitation
  • Anticipated prolonged resuscitation (e.g., ECPR candidacy) where central access will be needed regardless
02

Contraindications

  • Active CPR at the intended site preventing safe access (femoral line placement should not interrupt or interfere with ongoing compressions — choose a site and technique that coexists with compressions)
  • Known femoral vascular injury or prior femoral graft at the intended site (relative — choose alternate side or site)
  • Coagulopathy is a relative, not absolute, contraindication in the arrest setting given the urgency of the indication
03

Equipment

  • Central line kit (triple-lumen or appropriate for indication)
  • Ultrasound with linear probe if available and time allows
  • Chlorhexidine prep, sterile technique as feasible given the emergent context
  • Guidewire, dilator, catheter per Seldinger technique
04

Stepwise Technique

1
Choose the femoral vein as the default site during active arrest — it does not require interrupting compressions or airway management, unlike subclavian or internal jugular approaches.
2
Identify landmarks: femoral vein lies medial to the femoral artery, below the inguinal ligament (use ultrasound if immediately available without delaying access).
3
Insert the needle at a 30-45 degree angle, aspirating as you advance, aiming just medial to the palpated or visualized arterial pulse.
4
Confirm venous return (dark, non-pulsatile blood), thread the guidewire.
5
Make a small skin nick, dilate the tract.
6
Advance the catheter over the wire to the appropriate depth, remove the wire.
7
Aspirate and flush all ports, secure the catheter.
8
Obtain confirmatory imaging or clinical confirmation once the immediate resuscitation allows; reassess placement once the code concludes.
Femoral is the default during active compressions

It's the one site that doesn't compete with the airway or the person doing chest compressions — don't let the pursuit of ultrasound guidance delay access if landmark technique is faster and the operator is confident.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Arterial puncture — apply direct pressure, do not remove the needle blindly if committed to the wire without confirming venous placement first
  • Guidewire embolization if not held securely at all times
  • Infection risk elevated given compromised sterile technique in an emergent setting — plan for line exchange once the patient is stabilized
  • Delayed recognition of arterial cannulation if committed to dilation before confirming venous return
06

Pearls

Femoral access is the default during active compressions because it's the one site that doesn't compete with the airway or the person doing compressions.

Don't let the pursuit of ultrasound guidance delay access if landmark technique is faster and the operator is confident — use whichever gets you reliable access soonest.

Any line placed under emergent, imperfect sterile conditions should be flagged for exchange once the patient is stabilized.
