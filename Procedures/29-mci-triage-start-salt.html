Mass Casualty Triage — START / SALT Execution

The initial sort when casualties outstrip resources. Two frameworks, one discipline: sort fast, intervene minimally, keep moving, reassess constantly.

MASS CASUALTY
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Any incident where the number of casualties exceeds immediately available resources (mass casualty incident, MCI)
  • Applies at the scene during the initial sorting pass, before individualized patient care begins
  • START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) is the most widely used initial sort in the US prehospital system; SALT (Sort, Assess, Lifesaving interventions, Treatment/transport) is the current national model integrating a global sort step and limited lifesaving interventions
02

Contraindications

  • None — the framework applies whenever resources are outstripped by casualty volume; the judgment call is whether an MCI declaration is warranted, not whether to triage once declared
03

Equipment

  • Triage tags/tape (color-coded: Red/Immediate, Yellow/Delayed, Green/Minor, Black/Expectant or deceased)
  • Simple airway adjuncts for lifesaving interventions only (oral airway, occlusive dressing for open chest wound, tourniquet)
  • No diagnostic equipment, no IV starts, no detailed exams during the initial sort — this is a rapid pass, not treatment
04

Stepwise Technique

START
1
Direct all ambulatory patients to a designated area and tag them Green (Minor) — this immediately reduces the pool requiring individual assessment.
2
For each remaining patient, assess Respirations first: not breathing → open airway; still not breathing → Black (Expectant/Deceased). Breathing after repositioning, or RR >30/min → Red (Immediate). RR <30/min → proceed to perfusion.
3
Assess Perfusion (radial pulse present/absent, or cap refill): absent (or >2 sec) → control major hemorrhage if present, tag Red (Immediate). Present → proceed to mental status.
4
Assess Mental status (only if respirations and perfusion are within limits): unable to follow simple commands → Red (Immediate). Able to follow commands → Yellow (Delayed).
5
Move to the next patient — the entire assessment should take under 60 seconds per patient.
SALT
6
Sort: Global voice sort — call out for anyone who can walk to move to one area (assess last), then anyone who can wave or purposefully move to another area (assess second), leaving non-purposeful movers/obviously injured to be assessed first.
7
Assess each patient in that priority order (still, then waving, then walking), applying lifesaving interventions as you go rather than deferring.
8
Lifesaving interventions performed during assessment, not withheld: open airway, control major hemorrhage (tourniquet/wound packing), chest decompression for tension physiology, auto-injector antidotes if indicated (e.g., nerve agent exposure).
9
Assign triage category after interventions: obeys commands, has a pulse, no respiratory distress, hemorrhage controlled → likely to survive with current resources → Immediate or Delayed based on injury severity. Injuries incompatible with survival given available resources → Expectant. No signs of life after airway opened, no obvious reason for futility → Dead.
10
Reassess and re-triage as resources or patient status change — MCI triage is dynamic, not a single pass.
Global sort before individual assessment

The walk/wave/still voice sort does most of the categorization work before hands ever touch a patient — resist the pull to fully manage the first critical patient found at the expense of sorting the rest of the scene.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Getting pulled into treating one patient at the expense of the global sort — the initial pass is deliberately superficial
  • Failing to reassess — Yellow/Delayed patients can deteriorate; triage is iterative through the incident
  • Over-triage saturating resources — tagging too many patients Red degrades the ability to treat the truly immediate
  • Under-triage from anchoring on scene chaos — a quiet, still patient may be worse off than a loud one
  • Confusing the triage officer role with the treatment role — the job is to sort and move, not provide ongoing care to any single patient
06

Pearls

The global voice sort in SALT (walk/wave/still) does most of the categorization work before you ever lay hands on a patient.

Lifesaving interventions during SALT are deliberately limited to airway, hemorrhage, decompression, and antidotes — anything beyond belongs in the treatment phase.

Speak your tag category out loud as you assign it — this creates a running verbal count for incident command and catches your own errors in real time.

Whichever system your agency/region has adopted, the discipline is identical: sort fast, intervene minimally, keep moving.
