Symphysiotomy & Procedural Shoulder Dystocia Maneuvers

The escalation ladder for shoulder dystocia, ending in a rarely-needed but field-relevant last resort.

OBSTETRIC

01

Indications

  • Shoulder dystocia: failure of the fetal shoulders to deliver spontaneously after delivery of the head, often recognized by the "turtle sign" (head retracting against the perineum)
  • Symphysiotomy specifically reserved for shoulder dystocia refractory to standard maneuvers, in settings without immediate access to cesarean delivery
02

Contraindications

  • Symphysiotomy: any setting with timely access to cesarean delivery should default to that option instead — this is a resource-limited/field last resort, not a routine escalation step
  • Standard maneuvers have essentially no contraindications in true shoulder dystocia given the acute fetal risk
03

Equipment

  • None required for positional/manual maneuvers
  • Scalpel, local anesthetic if performing symphysiotomy
04

Stepwise Technique

Escalation sequence for shoulder dystocia
1
Call for help immediately and note the time — shoulder dystocia is a time-critical fetal emergency.
2
McRoberts maneuver: sharply flex the mother's hips against her abdomen, flattening the sacrum and rotating the pubic symphysis, often resolving the dystocia alone.
3
Apply suprapubic pressure simultaneously (not fundal pressure, which worsens impaction) directed downward and laterally to adduct the anterior shoulder.
4
If unresolved, proceed to internal maneuvers: deliver the posterior arm, or rotational maneuvers (Rubin or Woods screw) to rotate the fetal shoulders into an oblique plane.
5
Consider the Gaskin (all-fours) maneuver if the mother's position allows and other techniques have failed.
6
As a last resort in an unresolved dystocia with fetal compromise and no timely surgical option, consider deliberate clavicular fracture or, exceedingly rarely, symphysiotomy.
Symphysiotomy (last-resort technique)
7
Confirm all standard maneuvers have failed and cesarean delivery is not accessible in the necessary timeframe.
8
Infiltrate local anesthetic over the pubic symphysis if time and circumstances allow.
9
With the mother's legs supported and hips moderately abducted, make an incision through the skin and the fibrocartilaginous symphyseal joint at the midline.
10
Apply controlled pressure only as needed to create additional pelvic outlet space — this is a measured, not maximal, separation.
11
Proceed immediately with delivery of the impacted shoulder once adequate space is achieved.
12
Manage the symphyseal disruption post-delivery per orthopedic/surgical follow-up — this creates a significant maternal injury that requires definitive management afterward.
McRoberts plus suprapubic pressure resolves most cases

Never apply fundal pressure — it worsens impaction rather than relieving it. Symphysiotomy is a genuine last resort reserved for settings without timely cesarean access, not a routine escalation step.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Brachial plexus injury or clavicular/humeral fracture to the fetus from excessive traction during any maneuver — avoid fundal pressure and excessive axial traction throughout
  • Maternal perineal/vaginal trauma from internal maneuvers
  • Symphysiotomy: significant maternal pelvic instability, pain, and long-term morbidity — this is a genuinely last-resort technique with substantial maternal cost
  • Delayed escalation through the maneuver sequence in a truly refractory dystocia, worsening fetal hypoxic risk
06

Pearls

McRoberts plus suprapubic pressure resolves the large majority of shoulder dystocias — most cases never need to progress further down this list.

Never apply fundal pressure in shoulder dystocia — it worsens impaction rather than relieving it; suprapubic pressure is the correct external maneuver.

Symphysiotomy belongs in the field/resource-limited-setting category of this library specifically because it is almost never the right choice when a timely cesarean is available — know your setting's actual escalation pathway before assuming this technique applies.
