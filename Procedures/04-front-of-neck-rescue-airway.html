Front-of-Neck Rescue Airway in CICO

The decision framework that sits above the individual techniques — recognizing CICO fast, and choosing the right front-of-neck approach without hesitation.

AIRWAY
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Cannot Intubate, Cannot Oxygenate (CICO) confirmed after failed attempts at face mask ventilation, supraglottic airway placement, and tracheal intubation
  • Progressive desaturation despite optimized attempts at the above
  • Any scenario where the algorithm has been exhausted and oxygenation cannot be achieved by any other route
02

Contraindications

  • None — by definition, if the diagnosis of CICO is correct, a front-of-neck airway is indicated; the only real contraindication is misdiagnosing the situation as CICO when another maneuver would still work
03

Equipment

  • Pre-designated CICO/difficult airway cart, immediately available and known to the team
  • Scalpel-bougie-tube setup as the primary adult technique (see Cricothyrotomy entry)
  • Needle cricothyrotomy/jet ventilation setup for pediatric or anatomy-limited cases
  • Clear cognitive aid posted or immediately accessible (CICO algorithm card)
04

Stepwise Technique

1
Declare CICO out loud to the team the moment it is recognized — verbalizing the diagnosis is what triggers the algorithm shift and mobilizes help.
2
Call for the difficult airway/CICO cart immediately if not already open.
3
Default to the scalpel-bougie-tube (surgical cricothyrotomy) technique in adults as the fastest, most reliable front-of-neck approach — do not default to needle techniques or percutaneous kits when speed is the priority.
4
In pediatric patients or anatomy where surgical technique is disproportionately difficult, default to needle cricothyrotomy with jet ventilation instead.
5
Execute the chosen technique without further attempts at oral intubation or mask ventilation — going back to a failed technique wastes the only resource that matters, time.
6
Confirm oxygenation via the front-of-neck airway before considering the emergency resolved.
7
Debrief and plan definitive airway management (OR-based conversion, tracheostomy) once the patient is stabilized.
The hard part is the decision, not the scalpel

Rehearse saying "this is CICO, we are moving to a surgical airway" out loud in simulation — a pre-briefed team with a known default technique moves faster than the smartest individual improvising alone.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Delayed declaration of CICO — teams often persist with failing techniques past the point they should have escalated
  • Choosing a slower technique (percutaneous/Seldinger) when the situation calls for the fastest option (surgical)
  • Fixation on a single failed approach instead of moving decisively to the front-of-neck algorithm
  • Team communication breakdown — if CICO isn't declared out loud, help doesn't mobilize in time
06

Pearls

The hardest part of CICO management isn't the scalpel technique — it's the decision to commit to it. Practice saying “this is CICO, we are moving to a surgical airway” out loud in simulation so it isn't the first time in a real event.

This entry is the decision layer sitting above the individual techniques (see Cricothyrotomy) — know both, but know this framework first.

A pre-briefed team with a known cart location and a known default technique moves faster than the smartest individual improvising alone.
