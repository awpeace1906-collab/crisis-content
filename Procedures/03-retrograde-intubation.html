Retrograde Intubation

A backup technique for the difficult airway when fiberoptic and video laryngoscopy aren't available or aren't working — a wire from below guiding the tube from above.

AIRWAY
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Difficult airway where standard laryngoscopy and video-assisted techniques have failed or are unavailable
  • Significant blood or secretions obscuring the glottic view for fiberoptic technique
  • Limited mouth opening where a rigid scope cannot be introduced but the cricothyroid membrane is accessible
  • Resource-limited settings without fiberoptic or video laryngoscopy equipment
02

Contraindications

  • True CICO emergency — this technique is too slow; go directly to a surgical airway
  • Coagulopathy or overlying infection at the cricothyroid membrane puncture site (relative)
  • Distorted or unidentifiable anterior neck anatomy preventing safe membrane puncture
  • Any faster airway option is available and appropriate — retrograde intubation is a backup, not a first-line technique
03

Equipment

  • Introducer needle (similar to percutaneous cricothyrotomy kit)
  • Long guidewire or epidural catheter
  • Retrieval forceps or hemostat
  • Standard ETT
  • Local anesthetic if patient is awake/cooperative
04

Stepwise Technique

1
Identify and stabilize the cricothyroid membrane.
2
Puncture the membrane with the introducer needle, directed cephalad, confirming tracheal entry by air aspiration.
3
Thread the guidewire or epidural catheter through the needle, advancing it cephalad through the larynx and into the oropharynx.
4
Retrieve the wire from the mouth (or nose, depending on technique) using direct visualization, laryngoscopy, or forceps.
5
Thread the ETT over the retrieved wire in an antegrade fashion, advancing it down toward the larynx.
6
Advance the ETT into the trachea while withdrawing/releasing the wire at the neck puncture site as the tube passes the cords.
7
Once the tube is through the cords and into the trachea, remove the wire entirely.
8
Confirm placement (EtCO2, chest rise, auscultation), secure the tube, manage the neck puncture site.
Know where this sits on the urgency ladder

This technique is slower than fiberoptic or video laryngoscopy and far slower than a surgical airway — in a true CICO emergency, skip straight to cricothyrotomy rather than starting here.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Technique is time-consuming relative to other rescue options — poor choice when seconds matter most
  • Wire retrieval failure or loss of wire position during tube advancement
  • Bleeding at the puncture site obscuring the field
  • Tube may catch at the glottic inlet advancing over the wire — gentle rotation and steady wire tension help guide it through
  • False sense of security delaying escalation to a surgical airway if the technique is struggling
06

Pearls

This sits between fiberoptic technique and a surgical airway on the urgency spectrum — know your algorithm's place for it before you need it.

A firecracker-fast true CICO scenario should skip straight to cricothyrotomy; retrograde intubation belongs to the difficult-but-not-yet-critical airway.

Have a clear mental time limit for this technique — if it isn't progressing, abandon it for a surgical airway rather than persisting past the point of safety.
