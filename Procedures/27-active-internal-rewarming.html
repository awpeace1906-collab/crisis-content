Active Internal Rewarming (ECMO/CPB Crossover)

Core rewarming for severe hypothermia with cardiac instability — where extracorporeal circulation becomes both the rewarming method and the resuscitation.

ENVIRONMENTAL
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Severe hypothermia (core temperature typically <28°C) with cardiac instability, arrhythmia (including ventricular fibrillation), or cardiac arrest
  • Hypothermic cardiac arrest, where standard resuscitation and defibrillation are often ineffective until adequate rewarming is achieved — extracorporeal rewarming is the definitive management in this population
02

Contraindications

  • None absolute in hypothermic cardiac arrest with a survivable overall clinical picture (the guiding principle: "nobody is dead until warm and dead")
  • Injuries or comorbidities incompatible with survival regardless of rewarming (as with any ECMO candidacy decision)
03

Equipment

  • ECMO or cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) circuit and cannulation equipment (see ECMO Cannulation entry)
  • Continuous core temperature monitoring
  • Full resuscitation equipment, as CPR and standard ACLS measures continue in parallel during cannulation
04

Stepwise Technique

1
Continue high-quality CPR and standard resuscitation measures throughout — do not withhold compressions while awaiting rewarming capability.
2
Recognize that standard defibrillation and many ACLS medications are often ineffective below a core temperature threshold (commonly cited around 30°C) — limit repeated defibrillation attempts and medication administration per hypothermia-specific arrest protocols until adequate rewarming is underway.
3
Activate ECMO/CPB team and cannulate per the standard ECMO cannulation framework (see the ECMO Cannulation Prep & Activation entry), using this specifically as both circulatory support and a rewarming method.
4
Once on circuit, rewarm gradually and per protocol — controlled rewarming rate, avoiding the afterdrop and rewarming shock associated with overly rapid temperature correction.
5
Continue resuscitation efforts as core temperature rises, reattempting defibrillation and standard ACLS interventions once temperature thresholds are crossed per protocol.
6
Monitor closely for reperfusion/rewarming complications: arrhythmia, electrolyte shifts, and hemodynamic instability as peripheral vasodilation occurs with rewarming.
Nobody is dead until warm and dead

Profound hypothermia is neuroprotective, and standard defibrillation/ACLS medications are often ineffective below roughly 30°C — don't burn through hypothermia-specific attempt limits before rewarming capability is underway.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Afterdrop (continued core temperature decline immediately after rewarming initiation) from cold peripheral blood returning centrally
  • Rewarming shock from peripheral vasodilation outpacing volume status
  • Arrhythmia during the rewarming process, particularly as temperature crosses certain thresholds
  • Delayed recognition that standard resuscitation measures are unlikely to succeed without rewarming capability, delaying ECMO/CPB activation
06

Pearls

"Nobody is dead until warm and dead" is the governing principle — profound hypothermia is neuroprotective, and patients have survived prolonged arrest with intact neurologic outcomes after extracorporeal rewarming.

This is functionally the same activation and cannulation process as standard ECPR (see the ECMO Cannulation Prep & Activation entry), applied specifically to the hypothermic arrest population where the circuit does double duty as both circulatory support and the rewarming method itself.

Don't burn through your hypothermia-specific ACLS medication/defibrillation allowances early — most hypothermia protocols cap attempts below a temperature threshold precisely because they're unlikely to work until the myocardium is warm enough to respond.
