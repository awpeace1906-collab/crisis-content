Awake Fiberoptic Intubation

The controlled, spontaneously-breathing approach to the anticipated difficult airway — topicalize, sedate lightly, scope while the patient protects their own airway.

AIRWAY
⚡ TECHNIQUE Indications Contraindications Equipment Complications & Pitfalls Pearls References
01

Indications

  • Anticipated difficult airway (limited mouth opening, cervical spine immobility, airway mass/distortion, prior difficult intubation history)
  • Need to preserve spontaneous ventilation through the intubation process
  • Unstable cervical spine where direct laryngoscopy positioning is contraindicated
  • Upper airway pathology (tumor, abscess, angioedema) where induction could precipitate total obstruction
02

Contraindications

  • Uncooperative patient unable to tolerate awake instrumentation (relative — may require heavier sedation or an alternative plan)
  • Actively vomiting or high aspiration risk with an unprotected, un-topicalized airway
  • Rapidly deteriorating airway where the time cost of awake technique is not survivable — move to a faster rescue plan (surgical airway) instead
  • Known severe local anesthetic allergy limiting topicalization options (relative — alternative agents exist)
03

Equipment

  • Flexible fiberoptic bronchoscope with light source and monitor
  • Topical local anesthetic (nebulized and/or direct application: lidocaine 2-4%)
  • Atomizer or nebulizer device for topicalization
  • Antisialagogue (glycopyrrolate) given in advance to dry secretions and improve visualization
  • Light or titrated sedation agents (dexmedetomidine, low-dose midazolam/fentanyl) preserving spontaneous respiration
  • Appropriately sized ETT preloaded on the scope
  • Full difficult airway cart on standby
04

Stepwise Technique

1
Administer antisialagogue early enough to dry secretions before topicalization begins.
2
Topicalize the airway systematically: nares (if nasal approach), oropharynx, and progressively deeper structures using nebulized and direct local anesthetic application, often via a “spray as you go” technique through the scope's working channel.
3
Titrate light sedation to maintain a cooperative, spontaneously breathing patient — the goal is comfort and cooperation, not unconsciousness.
4
Preload the ETT onto the bronchoscope shaft.
5
Advance the scope through the chosen route (oral or nasal), maintaining midline orientation and continuously advancing toward the glottis under direct vision.
6
Identify the vocal cords and advance the scope tip through the glottis into the trachea, confirming tracheal rings and carina view.
7
Railroad the ETT over the scope into the trachea.
8
Confirm depth and position, withdraw the scope, confirm placement (EtCO2, bilateral breath sounds), secure the tube.
9
Only proceed to induction of general anesthesia once tracheal placement is confirmed — this is the entire safety rationale for the awake technique.
Sedation depth is the whole safety margin

The entire value of this technique is that the patient keeps breathing for themselves throughout — titrate sedation conservatively, since oversedation converts a controlled awake technique into an unplanned loss of airway.

05

Complications & Pitfalls

  • Inadequate topicalization causing patient discomfort, coughing, or laryngospasm during the procedure
  • Oversedation converting a spontaneously breathing awake technique into an unplanned loss of airway
  • Secretions or bleeding obscuring the fiberoptic view — antisialagogue and gentle technique mitigate this
  • Prolonged procedure time in a patient who cannot tolerate delay — recognize early when to abandon for a faster rescue plan
  • Esophageal misidentification if orientation is lost — maintain constant visual confirmation of airway structures
06

Pearls

The entire value of this technique is that the patient keeps breathing for themselves throughout — protect that at all costs by titrating sedation conservatively.

“Spray as you go” topicalization through the working channel keeps you from having to stop and re-dose separately — it's more efficient and better tolerated.

Have a clear abort criterion decided before you start: if topicalization or scope advancement isn't progressing within a reasonable window, move to the backup plan rather than persisting.

This is a skill that degrades fast without practice — simulation reps on a mannequin keep the scope-handling reflexes sharp between real cases.
