# Photo wishlist — where a real photograph beats a diagram

Running list for later. These are the places where the teaching is
**"recognize this when you see it"**, which line art cannot do.

Almost all of these are better shot by you than licensed from anywhere:
a phone photo of your own department's kit is more useful than a stock image
of a different manufacturer's, and the licensing is unambiguous because it's
yours. Nothing here is urgent — the text and diagrams stand on their own.

**Format when shooting:** landscape, plain dark background if possible (it sits
on a dark surface in-app), good light, nothing identifiable — no patients, no
faces, no name badges, no labels with institution or patient data. WebP or JPEG,
and I'll compress them into the asset budget.

---

## High value — the teaching genuinely fails without them

| # | Photo | Procedure | Why a diagram can't do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The sterile protective sleeve**, on its own and loaded onto a pacing wire | Transvenous pacing | "Load the sleeve first" is the single most-repeated error, and people skip it partly because they don't know what the part looks like. A photo makes it unmistakable. |
| 2 | **Cricothyrotomy set laid out** — #10 blade, bougie, 6.0 cuffed tube | Cricothyrotomy | The text says "have the 6.0 pre-identified." Seeing the three items together is the whole point; it's also the fastest way to check your own cart matches. |
| 3 | **Your institution's CICO cart, opened** | Cricothyrotomy, CICO | The text's strongest equipment advice is "open your kit once in daylight." A photo of *your actual cart* makes that concrete for everyone who'll use it. |
| 4 | **Commercial PDT kit contents, unpacked** | Tracheostomy | Same argument. Kits vary a lot by manufacturer and meeting one cold at 3 a.m. is the documented failure. |
| 5 | **Transvenous generator front panel**, with rate/output/sensitivity dials | Transvenous pacing | "Lower mV = more sensitive" is counterintuitive. Showing the actual dial layout removes an entire class of error. |

## Medium value — helpful, not load-bearing

| # | Photo | Procedure | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Balloon-tipped pacing wire, balloon inflated vs deflated | Transvenous pacing | Supports "up going in, down coming out." |
| 7 | Alligator clip attached to the **distal** wire terminal | Transvenous pacing | The proximal/distal distinction is the teaching; a photo settles it. |
| 8 | Pacing pads in true anterior-posterior placement, on a manikin | Pacing | Diagram probably suffices, but a photo shows realistic pad size vs. torso. |
| 9 | Finger thoracostomy / chest tube tray | Chest tube | Lower priority — a well-known setup. |
| 10 | Pelvic binder correctly positioned at greater trochanters | Pelvic binder | "Too high" is the classic error and is very visible in a photo. |

## Already sourced — no photo needed

**Anterior neck ultrasound** — four CC BY 4.0 images secured from Riveros-Perez
et al., *Healthcare* 2025. See `assets/licensed/README.md`. These cover the
transverse cricothyroid view, the longitudinal "string of pearls," and the
transverse views at cricoid and tracheal-ring level.

## Explicitly not wanted

**Cricothyrotomy or escharotomy performed on a patient or cadaver.** Consent and
licensing complications outweigh the teaching gain, and the landmark diagrams
serve better. Not a gap to fill.
